Ask most sales teams where a deal came from, and they'll tell you about the last thing that happened before it landed in Pipedrive — a form fill, a booked call, an inbound email.

Ask marketing the same question, and they'll point at a completely different set of numbers: ad platform dashboards, email open rates, landing page traffic.

Neither view is wrong. They're just both incomplete, because most attribution only looks at the last touchpoint — the one that happened to close the deal — and ignores everything that came before it.

---

## The Problem With Last-Click Attribution

Last-click attribution gives all the credit to whatever channel a prospect interacted with right before converting.

In practice, that usually means:

- A prospect clicks a paid ad, reads three pages on your site, forgets about you for two weeks, then comes back through a Google search and fills in a form. Last-click attribution credits organic search — the ad that actually started things gets nothing.
- An email campaign gets someone to open an email and click through, but they'd already visited the site twice before that from a LinkedIn post. The email gets full credit; LinkedIn gets none.
- A deal is created from a referral, but the prospect had already been reading your content and clicking your ads for a month beforehand.

None of this is visible if you're only looking at the last touch. It quietly rewards whichever channel happens to close the loop and hides the channels doing the early-stage work of actually generating interest.

---

## What Full-Journey Attribution Looks Like

Full-journey attribution captures every touchpoint a prospect has with your business — every ad click, every website visit, every email interaction — from the very first one, and connects it to what eventually happens in your CRM.

That means being able to see, for a single prospect:

- The very first ad, email or page that brought them to your site
- Every page they visited and how long they spent on each one
- The point at which they identified themselves (a form fill, a signup, a booked call)
- Every Pipedrive activity and deal-stage change that followed

Instead of one attribution data point, you get the whole story — and both first-touch and last-touch data, so you can credit the channel that started the conversation as well as the one that finished it.

---

## Why This Matters for Pipedrive Users

Pipedrive is excellent at managing the sales process once a deal exists, but by default it has no visibility into what happened *before* a lead came in — which ad they clicked, which emails they opened, which pages they read.

That gap creates real problems:

1. **Marketing can't prove what's working.** Without connecting ad spend to actual pipeline outcomes inside the CRM, campaign performance gets judged on clicks and form fills rather than the deals they actually influence.
2. **Sales reps have no context.** A rep opening a new deal has no idea whether the prospect has been reading the site for a month or just landed five minutes ago from a cold ad.
3. **Reporting requires manual reconciliation.** Connecting ad platform data to CRM outcomes usually means exporting from multiple systems and matching records by hand — a process most teams don't have time to repeat regularly.

---

## Bringing Attribution Directly Into Pipedrive

The most useful place for attribution data to live isn't a separate dashboard nobody checks — it's on the Deal and Person records your team already opens every day.

A tracking layer that captures ad clicks and website behaviour, links it to a Pipedrive Person once they convert, and writes first-touch and last-touch channel, source, medium and campaign onto custom fields means:

- Sales reps see attribution context without leaving Pipedrive
- Marketing can filter and report on real pipeline outcomes using Pipedrive's own reporting tools
- The full visual journey — every touchpoint, in order — is one click away from any Deal
- None of it requires exporting data or maintaining a separate system

---

## Practical Use Cases

This kind of setup is useful for:

- Businesses running paid ads who want to know which campaigns are actually generating pipeline, not just clicks
- Teams sending email campaigns who want to see how email fits into a prospect's wider journey
- Sales teams who want context on a lead before picking up the phone
- Marketing and sales teams who currently disagree about which channels are working
- Any Pipedrive user who wants attribution data without adding a new tool to their day-to-day workflow

---

## A Recent Example

Automated Sales recently built a prospect journey attribution tool that does exactly this — tracking ad clicks, website visits and email activity, linking it to Pipedrive contacts, and writing first-touch and last-touch attribution directly onto Deal and Person records.

It combines that tracked activity with Pipedrive's own sales activity into a single timeline, and includes a dashboard for reviewing channel performance across an entire account.

---

## Final Thoughts

Last-click attribution isn't wrong, exactly — it's just answering a narrower question than most teams think it is.

The channels that start a conversation are often just as important as the ones that close it, and without full-journey attribution, that early-stage work stays invisible.

For Pipedrive users, the most practical fix isn't a separate analytics platform — it's getting that attribution data onto the records the sales team already relies on.
